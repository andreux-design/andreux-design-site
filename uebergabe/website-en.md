Senior Product Designer

I design products and the systems they stand on.

I like to accompany a product from research through concept to implementation. Along the way I see where work repeats itself, and I build the system for it, out of components, tokens, rules and checks. At first that is extra effort. As soon as several people work on one product, it pays off. Everyone builds on the same foundation, the result stays consistent, and a change in one place applies everywhere. It started more than ten years ago, long before AI tools like Claude Code, with a report that came out of an input form at the push of a button. Today I build such systems with AI models that I brief like employees, and my knowledge of software development helps me with that.

Research, concept, prototype, handover to development, support through to launch.

### GoTiger App

#### Rapid delivery service for Asian groceries

GoTiger delivered Asian groceries in Berlin, and I designed the delivery service's app. One problem lay outside the app. Asian supermarkets are often chaotic, and without prior knowledge you don't know what you can even buy there. So every product detail page explained the product, and recipes put the ingredients into the basket with one click, matched to the portion size. What you already had at home, you deselected. There was also a swipe game for finding the right recipe. All of it went live.

I started with a survey of the target group, and later user tests in interviews were added. It wasn't highly scientific. But it was enough to see what needed improving. When a colleague left, I took over her tasks as well and from then on briefed the external development team.

### filo

#### Messages and files in one place

filo, my bachelor's thesis, came out of everyday work. You look for a message about a project and no longer remember where it was. Did it come by email or via Slack? And the file that goes with it, was that on the drive or in some chat after all? Searching like that costs time, and when things get hectic, it turns into stress. I wanted an interface that brings everything together in one place, without everyone having to use the same tool. What surprised me was how many people feel the same.

I built the interface as a clickable Figma prototype that simulates its states through 271 variables without a backend. I evaluated the prototype with 23 participants in moderated online sessions. Even participants with little technical experience could handle it. That pleased me. A control panel I built for myself in my current role follows a similar idea.

### colibre

#### Brand and app prototype

colibre was a study project in the summer semester of 2022, in the course Brand Design Development. First came the brand, a brand compass with the three axes Lifestyle, Innovation and Connection and with values against which every further decision could be measured. Then came the product. All of us in the team liked hiking, so we asked ourselves what we were missing out there. That is how the concept of an outdoor app came about, with a map, routes, a shop for equipment and a chat with people who had walked the same route. The Colispot, the button in the middle of the navigation, was the core. With it you could mark a beautiful place on the map and share it. The three axes appeared again in the app, the shop as Lifestyle, the Colispot as Innovation, the chat as Connection. And the slanted edge of the logo ran through buttons, switches and price tags, so the brand stayed visible right into the controls.

As the most experienced in the team of three, I divided the tasks by skill and built the prototype myself, interactive in Figma. It went no further, it was a study project.

I build design systems, from design tokens across several layers to nested components and flows, with everything Figma offers for it.

### A building-block library for automated pages

#### Layout templates that became building blocks

At the moment I work at a performance marketing agency, where I am the only designer and responsible for building the funnels for our clients: landing pages with their copy, lead magnet PDFs and ad creatives. There, I first set up layout templates by hand. Each client got a small design system in Figma, only as far as they needed it.

Those templates have become 26 building blocks, from which pages are now generated automatically. If a client has no design system yet, or only an inadequate one, a new one is created at project start according to rules and applied to the building blocks. What I had built by hand has thus become the foundation of machine production.

### A library for Volkswagen

#### Figma components for the ID. Buzz and the California

In 2024, at a digital agency in Munich, I built the websites for two Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles models, the ID. Buzz and the California. The manufacturer had documented its components, but in our team they were missing as Figma components. So that we would not have to recreate them again and again, I built an internal library in Figma, piece by piece.

My colleagues, who built the other Volkswagen pages, were grateful. They could have come up with that themselves, they said, it was so obvious.

### A design system made of tokens

#### Switched to another brand with a few clicks

That same year, on my own initiative, I built a design system from design tokens, because the agency's white-label system we used to build client projects was spread across several files. The new system switches to another brand with a few clicks and carries light and dark mode.

When I presented it, everyone was baffled at first, and how it worked technically was not immediately clear. So I showed with a demo library what the system did. Afterwards the agency's leads talked to me one on one to understand it in detail. The demo library stayed with the agency. Whether the system is in use today, I don't know. My version of the “one conference” app, on the other hand, was in use at the agency's annual internal conference. I redesigned the app for mobile devices and desktop and added features. For that, I built a prototype in Figma with tokens and variables that carried the flows itself, and handed it over to the in-house developers.

In code, compilers and tests tell you whether it's right. For design and text there is no compiler, so someone who knows the craft has to write the rules and the checks.

The rules have an old origin. I have seen projects signed off by taste, first internally and then by the client, every time with many rounds. In the end, a lot of it violated the craft rules of design, texts, for instance, were longer than a reader can bear. So I set criteria by which work is judged: my own rules for text and design, and a checklist. The simplest rule fixes a screen width and renders a screenshot, so that everyone talks about the same view.

### A system for entire funnels

#### A wizard from briefing to landing page

On these rules now stands a system built with Claude Code that produces entire funnels. For a client, a briefing is stored, and from it the system builds a page with suitable sections and text. It becomes visible in a wizard that I designed and built. The idea is that anyone on the team can create a landing page with a few clicks. The texts are on the left as a form, the preview of the page on the right, switchable to the mobile view. Anyone who wants to put in more effort can add, remove or move sections. The overall layout is set by a template. Accessibility is a condition for approval, not rework.

By hand, as I remember it, the first build of a page alone took half a day to a full day, without text and without the mobile version. With the wizard, the first version, text included, is ready after about five minutes, and corrections come on top. The wizard is finished but still runs locally on my machine, and the move into the shared system is pending.
