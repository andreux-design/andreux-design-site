Senior Product Designer

I design products and the systems they stand on.

I like to accompany a product from research through concept to implementation. Along the way I see where work repeats itself, and I build the system for it, out of components, tokens, rules and checks. At first that is extra effort. As soon as several people work on one product, it pays off. Everyone builds on the same foundation, the result stays consistent, and a change in one place applies everywhere. It started more than ten years ago with a report that came out of an input form at the push of a button. Long before AI. Today I build such systems with models that I brief like employees, and my knowledge of software development helps me with that.

04/2022 to 05/2023 GoTiger, Berlin

GoTiger App

Rapid delivery service for Asian groceries

GoTiger delivered Asian groceries in Berlin, and I designed the delivery service's app. One problem lay outside the app. Asian supermarkets are often chaotic, and without prior knowledge you don't know what you can even buy there. So every product detail page explained the product, and recipes put the ingredients into the basket with one click, matched to the portion size. What you already had at home, you deselected. There was also a swipe game for finding the right recipe. All of it went live.

Approach: I started with a survey of the target group, and later user tests in interviews were added. It wasn't highly scientific. But it was enough to see what needed improving. When a colleague left, I took over her tasks as well and from then on briefed the external development team.

10/2024 to 03/2025 Bachelor's thesis

Filo

Filo, my bachelor's thesis, came out of everyday work. You look for a message about a project and no longer remember where it was. Did it come by email or via Slack? And the file that goes with it, was that on the drive or in some chat after all? Searching like that costs time, and when things get hectic, it turns into stress. I wanted an interface that brings everything together in one place, without everyone having to use the same tool. What surprised me was how many people feel the same.

Result: I built the interface as a clickable Figma prototype that simulates its states through 271 variables without a backend. I evaluated the prototype with 23 participants in moderated online sessions. Even participants with little technical experience could handle it. That pleased me. A control panel I built for myself in my current role follows a similar idea.

Summer semester 2022 Study project

colibre

Brand and app prototype

colibre was a study project in the summer semester of 2022, in the course Brand Design Development. First came the brand, a brand compass with the three axes Lifestyle, Innovation and Connection and with values against which every further decision could be measured. Then came the product. All of us in the team liked hiking, so we asked ourselves what we were missing out there. That is how the concept of an outdoor app came about, with a map, routes, a shop for equipment and a chat with people who had walked the same route. The Colispot, the button in the middle of the navigation, was the core. With it you could mark a beautiful place on the map and share it. The three axes appeared again in the app, the shop as Lifestyle, the Colispot as Innovation, the chat as Connection. And the slanted edge of the logo ran through buttons, switches and price tags, so the brand stayed visible right into the controls.

Approach: As the most experienced in the team of three, I divided the tasks by skill and built the prototype myself, interactive in Figma. It went no further, it was a study project.

Since 12/2025 At the agency

A building-block library for automated pages

In my current role I first set up layout templates by hand. Each client got a small design system in Figma, only as far as they needed it.

Result: Those templates have become 26 building blocks, from which pages are now generated automatically. If a client has no design system yet, one is now created at project start according to rules and applied to the building blocks. What I had built by hand has thus become the foundation of machine production.

2024 Digital agency, Munich

A library for Volkswagen

In 2024, at a digital agency in Munich, I built the websites for two Volkswagen commercial vehicle models, the ID. Buzz and the California. The manufacturer had documented its components but had not set them up as Figma components. I saw the gap. Piece by piece, I built an internal library in Figma.

Result: The colleagues who were also building Volkswagen pages were grateful. They could have come up with that themselves, they said, it was so obvious.

2024 Digital agency, Munich

A design system made of tokens

That same year, on my own initiative, I built a design system from design tokens, because the agency's white-label system we used to build client projects was spread across several files. The new system switches to another brand with a few clicks and carries light and dark mode.

Result: When I presented it, everyone was baffled at first, and how it worked technically was not immediately clear. So I showed with a demo library what the system did. Afterwards the agency's leads talked to me one on one to understand it in detail. The demo library stayed with the agency. Whether the system is in use today, I don't know.

AI-supported systems

In code, compilers and tests tell you whether it's right. For design and text there is no compiler, so someone who knows the craft has to write the rules and the checks.

The rules have an old origin. I have seen projects signed off by taste, first internally and then by the client, every time with many rounds. In the end, a lot of it violated the craft rules of design, texts, for instance, were longer than a reader can bear. So I set criteria by which work is judged: my own rules for text and design, and a checklist. The simplest rule fixes a screen width and renders a screenshot, so that everyone talks about the same view.

Since 12/2025 At the agency

A system for entire advertising campaigns

On these rules now stands a system built with Claude Code that produces entire advertising campaigns. For a client, a briefing is stored, then someone starts production, and text and page are created together with the mobile version. Once the text is done, a review view opens, the form for changes on the left, the preview of the page on the right. Media can be swapped, the layout comes from the template. Text and page are approved separately, and accessibility is a condition for both, not rework.

Result: By hand, the first build of a page alone took half a day to a full day, without text and without the mobile version. Today the first version, text included, is ready after about five minutes, and corrections come on top.

What I'm looking for

What I'm looking for is an environment with knowledgeable people, where work is done thoroughly and not just quickly and superficially. I'm looking for something long-lived, where care pays off, and a field in which my work matters. I like having the whole process in view, and doing that with a team, preferably on site, though I'm used to remote work as well. Pure execution is not what I'm after. When I have freedom and the necessary resources and can decide for myself, the work usually turns out well. I gladly take advice and criticism when they're constructive. In study projects I have led teams, and I can assess people and put them to good use. Senior in my field or a first step into leadership, either suits me. Leadership is something I want to learn properly. And more about AI.
