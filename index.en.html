I design the process, not just the result.

I have been working since 2010, designing since 2011, and doing UX since my studies. One thing runs through all of it. Where work repeats itself, I build the process for it and check before anything goes out. More than ten years ago I built a report that came out of an input form at the push of a button. Long before AI. In between, I enjoy accompanying a product from concept through research to implementation.

Built unasked

In 2024, at a digital agency in Munich, I built the websites for two Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles models, the ID. Buzz and the California. The manufacturer had documented its components but had not set them up as Figma components. I saw the gap. Piece by piece, I built an internal library in Figma. The colleagues who were also building Volkswagen pages were grateful. They could have come up with that themselves, they said, it was so obvious.

That same year, on my own initiative, I built a design system from design tokens, because the agency's white-label system we used to build client projects was cumbersome and spread across several files. The new system switches to another brand with a few clicks and carries light and dark mode. When I presented it, everyone was baffled at first, and how it worked technically was not immediately clear. So I showed with a demo what it does. Afterwards the agency's leads talked to me one on one to understand it in detail. The demo library I had built for it, I left with them as a parting gift. Whether the system is in use today, I don't know.

04/2022 to 05/2023 GoTiger, Berlin

GoTiger App

Rapid delivery service for Asian groceries

GoTiger delivered Asian groceries in Berlin, and I designed the app for it. One problem lay outside the app: Asian supermarkets are often chaotic, and without prior knowledge you don't know what you can even buy there. So every product detail page explained the product, and recipes put the ingredients straight into the basket, by portion size and by what the warehouse had in stock. Sometimes you already had an ingredient and didn't need to order it. On top came a swipe game for finding the right recipe. All of it went live.

Approach: I started with a survey of the target group; later, user tests in interviews were added. It wasn't highly scientific. But it was enough to see what needed improving. When a colleague left, I took over her tasks as well and from then on briefed the external development team.

10/2024 to 03/2025 Bachelor's thesis

Filo

Filo, my bachelor's thesis, came out of everyday work. You look for a message about a project: did it come by email or via Slack? And the file that goes with it is on the drive or in some chat. I wanted an interface that brings both together in one place. What surprised me was how many people feel the same and how much time is wasted searching, especially when things get hectic.

Result: I built the interface as a clickable Figma prototype that simulates its states through 271 variables without a backend; I evaluated it with 23 participants in moderated online sessions. Even participants with little technical experience could handle it. That pleased me. The control panel that runs production in my current role follows a similar idea.

AI-supported systems

Recurring work gets a process from me: rules, templates, checks. I used to brief developers, now I brief models. The same skill.

The reason for these rules is an old one. I have seen projects signed off by taste, first internally and then by the client, many rounds both times, and in the end the result was not good. A lot of it was even wrong in design terms, because it was judged by taste instead of by professional criteria. So I set criteria by which work is judged, to end the endless back and forth: my own rules for text and design, and a checklist.

What I'm looking for
