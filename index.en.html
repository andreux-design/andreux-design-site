Senior Product Designer

I design the process, not just the result.

I have seen projects signed off by taste, first internally and then by the client, many rounds both times, and in the end the result still was not good. A lot of it was even wrong in design terms, because taste was never judged on professional grounds. So I set the criteria by which work has been judged ever since, to end the endless back and forth. They include a set of rules, my own for text and design, and a checklist.

Saw a gap, filled it

In 2024, at a digital agency in Munich, I built the websites for two Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles models, the ID. Buzz and the California. The manufacturer had documented its components but had not set them up as Figma components. Anyone who worked with those components could have done that. I noticed there was a gap and, piece by piece, assembled the components into an internal library. The colleagues who were also building Volkswagen pages said they wondered why they had not done something like that themselves, it was so obvious. And they were grateful.

That same year, on my own initiative, I built a design system from design tokens, because the agency's white-label system we used to build client projects was cumbersome and spread across several files. It switches to another brand with a few clicks and carries light and dark mode. When I presented it, everyone was baffled at first. How it worked technically, they had not yet understood. So in the presentation I showed a demo of what it does, and afterwards the leads there talked to me one on one so I could explain it in detail. The demo library I had built for it, I left with them. The design system was my last big thing there, a farewell gift of sorts. Whether it is in use today, I don't know.

04/2022 to 05/2023 GoTiger, Berlin

GoTiger App

Rapid delivery service for Asian groceries

GoTiger delivered Asian groceries in Berlin, and I designed the app for it. One problem lay outside the app: Asian supermarkets are often chaotic, and without prior knowledge you don't know what you can even buy there. So every product detail page explained what the product was, and the recipes put the ingredients straight into the basket, by portion size and by what the warehouse had in stock. Sometimes you already had an ingredient and didn't need to order it. On top came a swipe game for finding the right recipe. All of it went live.

Approach: I started with a survey of the target group; later, user tests in interviews were added. It wasn't highly scientific. But it was enough to see what needed improving. When a colleague left later on, I took over her tasks as well and from then on briefed the external development team.

10/2024 to 03/2025 Bachelor's thesis

Filo

Filo, my bachelor's thesis, came out of everyday work. You look for a message about a project: did it come by email or via Slack? And the file that goes with it is on the drive or in some chat. I wanted an interface that brings both together in one place. What surprised me was how many people feel the same and how much time is wasted searching, especially when things get hectic.

Result: I built it as a clickable Figma prototype that simulates its states through 271 variables without a backend; I evaluated it with 23 participants in moderated online sessions. It was nice to see that even people with little technical knowledge could handle it. The interface that runs production in my current role is something quite similar.

What I'm looking for

What I'm looking for is an environment with knowledgeable people, where work is done thoroughly, without rush, rush. I like having the whole process in view, from concept through research to implementation, and doing that in a team, not alone. When I have room to decide for myself and the resources to go with it, things usually turn out well; I gladly take advice. In study projects I have led teams. Leadership is something I still want to learn properly. And more about AI.
